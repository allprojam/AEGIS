# Solana vs Ethereum: testing the "better asymmetric bet" claim

## 1. The liquidity claim — PARTIALLY TRUE, and the split matters

**24h DEX volume, measured live:**

| chain | 24h DEX volume |
|---|---|
| **Solana** | **$2,057M** |
| Ethereum L1 | $1,098M |
| Base (ETH L2) | $1,306M |
| Arbitrum (ETH L2) | $157M |

- **Solana vs Ethereum L1 alone: Solana wins, 1.9x.** His claim is correct on this framing.
- **Solana vs Ethereum L1 + major L2s: $2,057M vs $2,561M — the ETH ecosystem wins.**

So the claim is true or false depending on whether you count Base. And Base is the interesting case:
it is an Ethereum L2 by technology but a **Coinbase** chain by ownership and user base. That is the
real structural question his framing skips — not "is liquidity fragmented," but **whether an L2's
volume accrues to the ETH asset at all.**

## 2. The fragmentation claim — TRUE, but much smaller than implied

**DeFi TVL:**

| | TVL |
|---|---|
| Ethereum L1 | **$54.24B** |
| Ethereum L2s (all) | **$8.22B** |
| Ethereum ecosystem | $62.46B |
| Solana | $6.79B |

The L2s hold only **13%** of the ecosystem's value. So "ETH has fractured liquidity across layers"
is real as a phenomenon, but **the L1 still retains 87% of the value.** Fragmentation is a
rounding error against the scale difference: Ethereum's ecosystem carries **9x** Solana's TVL.

The honest reading: fragmentation is a genuine *structural* drag on ETH's ability to capture
activity, but it is not currently reflected in value. This is a *forward-looking* bearish argument
about ETH, not a description of present weakness.

## 3. Performance, 3 years (958 days, matched window)

| | return | max drawdown | from 3y high | last |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SOL** | **+406%** | **-76%** | -54% | $120.68 |
| ETH | +56% | -68% | -44% | $2,711.03 |

**SOL/ETH ratio: +223% over three years.** The relative bet paid enormously.

## 4. Is it "asymmetric"? — THE MEASURED ANSWER

This is the cleanest result. Monthly capture ratios, 36-month sample:

```
ETH UP   months (n=17):  SOL +23.8%   ETH +20.2%   upside capture  1.18x
ETH DOWN months (n=19):  SOL  -9.4%   ETH -12.3%   downside capture 0.76x

correlation 0.68
SOL ann vol 91%   ETH ann vol 73%
```

**Upside capture 1.18x against downside capture 0.76x.** Solana participates *more* when ETH rises
and *less* when ETH falls. That is the textbook definition of favourable asymmetry, and it is
measured, not asserted.

**His thesis survives this test.**

## 5. But "asymmetric" must be qualified — three ways

**(a) The downside is not limited.** SOL's own max drawdown over the window is **-76%**, worse
than ETH's -68%. The asymmetry is *relative to ETH*, not absolute. Both assets can lose
three-quarters of their value. A -76% drawdown is not a limited-downside bet.

**(b) The sample is thin.** 36 months, n=17 up and n=19 down months. This is a directionally real
result consistent with higher-beta behaviour, but the exact capture ratios should not be treated as
stable parameters. The ratio whipsawed: **2024 +21%, 2025 -27%, 2026 +5%.** Anyone who bought the
ratio at its 2025 high is still down substantially.

**(c) Correlation of 0.68 means a third of SOL's variance is independent.** That cuts both ways —
it is why the asymmetry exists, and it also means SOL can collapse for reasons that have nothing to
do with ETH.

## 6. Verdict

**On the liquidity claim:** half right. Solana beats Ethereum L1 on DEX volume; it does not beat
Ethereum L1 + L2s. The fragmentation he describes is real but small (13% of value).

**On "better asymmetric bet":** the data supports him. Measured upside capture exceeds downside
capture (1.18x vs 0.76x) and the relative bet returned +223% over three years. This is the first
claim in this entire research effort that survived testing in the *affirmative*.

**But the honest framing is not "SOL is asymmetric."** It is: **SOL is a higher-beta expression of
the same trade, with genuinely favourable relative skew — and absolute downside that is still
catastrophic.** The asymmetry is a reason to prefer SOL *if you have already decided to hold the
sector*, not a reason to hold the sector.
