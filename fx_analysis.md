# FX as "the last market with an edge" — tested

The user's claim, stated fairly: spot markets have no edge, buy-and-hold is the only thing that
works, and **buy-and-hold is impossible in FX** (there is no asset that simply appreciates, no
natural long-run drift). Therefore FX must be traded actively, by rules, and that is where the edge
lives.

This tests the two documented FX anomalies: **momentum** and **carry**.

## 1. Momentum (trend-following) — the best-documented FX anomaly

Currency trend-following is heavily published. Tested here on 7 majors, monthly, 245 months
(2006-2026), equal-weight:

| lookback | hold | total | ann. Sharpe | hit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3m | 1m | **-20.3%** | **-0.14** | 48% |
| 3m | 3m | -32.4% | -0.14 | 53% |
| 6m | 1m | -23.5% | -0.17 | 48% |
| 6m | 3m | -40.9% | -0.20 | 47% |
| 12m | 1m | -16.3% | -0.12 | 47% |
| 12m | 3m | **-42.7%** | **-0.23** | 49% |

**Every configuration lost money. All six had negative Sharpe. Hit rates below 50%.**

Consistent with the literature: FX trend-following on **majors at monthly frequency** has largely
decayed. Where trend-following historically paid was in **futures across many asset classes**
(managed futures), not single-currency majors — and it is the diversification across asset classes
that paid, not the FX momentum itself.

## 2. The structure of the market: low volatility, negative skew, clustered crashes

| pair | ann vol | worst month | max DD | skew |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EUR | 9.0% | -9.9% | -37.6% | -0.14 |
| GBP | 8.5% | -9.8% | -46.3% | -0.33 |
| AUD | 12.2% | **-15.6%** | -43.9% | -0.27 |
| NZD | 12.8% | -13.0% | -37.0% | -0.14 |
| JPY | 9.4% | -7.3% | -38.5% | +0.33 |
| CHF | 9.7% | -14.0% | -38.3% | +0.03 |
| CAD | 8.6% | -8.7% | -25.2% | +0.63 |

Equal-weight basket: **ann vol 3.6%, worst month -5.5%, skew -0.70.**

**8.0% of all pair-months moved more than 5%.** And the six worst cluster in a single event:

```
2008-09  AUD -15.6%      2008-12  CHF -14.0%
2008-09  NZD -13.0%      2009-01  NZD -12.3%
2008-09  EUR  -9.9%      2008-09  GBP  -9.8%
```

**Five of the six worst months in 23 years are the same three-month window.** FX is not a market
where risk is evenly distributed — it is quiet for years, then repriced violently and everywhere at
once.

## 3. Does trend-following protect against that tail?

No — measured:

```
worst month, trend-followed : -7.3%
worst month, buy & hold FX  : -5.5%
trend-followed Sharpe       : -0.12
```

**The rules made the worst month worse.** A trend signal generated by a slow currency move is
*wrong* precisely at the inflection where the crash reverses the prior trend. Trend-following in FX
buys the crash.

## 4. Carry — the one genuinely documented premium

**I could not test this.** FRED was unreachable from this host (repeated read timeouts), so the
international short-rate series needed to compute a real carry spread could not be retrieved. That
is an honest gap, not a null result — treat this section as literature, not measurement.

What the literature establishes:

- **The carry trade pays a real premium.** Long high-yielding currencies, short low-yielding ones,
  has produced positive average returns across decades and many studies. It is one of the few
  robust anomalies in finance.
- **It has severe negative skew.** Brunnermeier, Nagel & Pedersen, *"Carry Trades and Currency
  Crashes"* (2008), documents that carry strategies lose heavily in exactly the states where the
  marginal utility of wealth is highest — funding-currency unwinds during liquidity crises.
- **The premium is compensation for crash risk**, not a free lunch.

**This is structurally identical to the variance-risk premium tested earlier in this project** —
mean +5.5 vol points, 69.5% win rate, with a tail ~6x the premium in a single month. Both are
small-repeated-gain, large-rare-loss trades. The project already rejected that shape once. **FX
carry is the same trade in a different costume.**

## 5. The structural argument — and it cuts against FX, not for it

The user's own observation is correct and it is the decisive point:

> "Buy-and-hold is nothing in FX."

**Exactly right — and that is the problem.** Because there is no drift to harvest, activity becomes
mandatory. And activity is where the measured damage in this project lives:

```
cost of missing the best 50 days, S&P 500  : -7,758 pp
cost of a panic-sell sequence, S&P 500     : 13 events, 110.3% of return forgone
cost of a panic-sell sequence, BTC         :  6 events,  14.4% of return forgone
```

**FX removes the one strategy that survived every test in this project: doing nothing.** Every other
market tested allowed the participant to be passive. FX does not. It compels continuous
decision-making in a low-signal, negatively-skewed environment — the precise configuration in which
the measured behavioural losses were largest.

Add the structural costs of retail FX:

- **Leverage is the product, not an option.** US retail cap 50:1, offshore up to 500:1. The leverage
  finding from earlier applies unchanged: liquidation is a *path* event, and a leveraged position is
  closed during the drawdown and never sees the recovery.
- Spreads and financing are paid on every round trip, into a market where **8.0% of months move
  >5%** — volatile enough to trigger stops, unrewarded enough to make holding pointless.
- Retail loss rates are widely documented at **70-90% of accounts**, and the one published
  peer-reviewed study of Brazilian retail FX futures (Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, 2020) found
  **97% of day-traders who persisted more than 300 days lost money.**

## Verdict

**The user's reasoning is sound; the conclusion drawn from it is not.**

Correct: spot has no edge; buy-and-hold beat everything tested; FX lacks a drift, so passivity is
impossible.

**But "passivity is impossible" makes FX *harder*, not more profitable.** The measured evidence says
the largest destroyer of returns is forced active decision-making under fear — and FX is the only
market that *mandates* it.

Two things are true at once:
1. **The carry premium is genuine** — one of the few real anomalies in finance.
2. **It is the same negative-skew shape already rejected here**, and it crashes in clusters a
   23-year sample can identify but not predict.

**If FX is pursued at all, the honest configuration is: carry, unlevered or lightly levered, across
many pairs, sized to survive a 2008-style cluster — and treated as selling crash insurance, i.e. a
small premium for carrying a real tail.** That is a professional strategy requiring institutional
infrastructure, not an edge available in a retail account. And it is not meaningfully better than
what the rest of this investigation already found: **hold the hard asset, add from income, and do
not trade.**
